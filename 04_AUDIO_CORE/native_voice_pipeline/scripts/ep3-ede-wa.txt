Báwo ni, my beautiful people. Sisi Lola dey here, and today my heart dey full. We go talk about something wey dey precious to me. Ẹ̀dè wa. Our language.

You see, when I dey small, my mama go tell me, "Ọmọ mi, má gbàgbé èdè yín. Èdè ni ìdánimọ̀." My child, no forget your language. Language na your identity.

That time I no understand. I just want to speak fine English like the children for TV. I dey shy when papa speak Yoruba for outside. But now I done grow, I sabi say my mama was right.

When you lose your language, you dey lose part of your soul. The proverbs wey our ancestors carry from generation to generation, the songs wey our mothers sing while dem dey grind pepper, the prayers wey our fathers pray, all of am dey live inside the language.

So I dey beg you today. Speak Yoruba to your pikin. Speak Igbo. Speak Hausa. Speak Edo, Tiv, Ijaw, Efik. Speak the Pidgin wey unite us all. No let am die.

Èdè wa, ìgbéraga wa. Our language, our pride. When your child fit greet grandmother for Yoruba, that na real wealth. That money no fit buy.

Sisi Lola love una die. Ẹ má jẹ́ kí ó kú. No let am die. Speak am proud. Mo nífẹ̀ẹ́ yín. Bye for now, my people.
